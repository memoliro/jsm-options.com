Covered call strategy for beginners

Search focus: covered call strategy · covered call for beginners · covered call payoff · covered call example · sell covered call

A covered call means you own at least 100 shares of a stock or ETF and you sell one call option against those shares. You collect a premium (cash credit) up front. In return, you agree to sell your shares at the call’s strike if the buyer exercises.

Traders use covered calls when they are neutral to moderately bullish: happy to hold the shares, willing to cap some upside, and interested in income from the option premium.

Structure

  • Long 100 shares (or more, in 100-share lots per call sold)
  • Short 1 call (same underlying), typically out-of-the-money or at-the-money

Payoff intuition

  • Max profit ≈ (strike − stock purchase price) + premium received (if shares are called away at the strike)
  • Downside is still mainly the stock — the premium only cushions the fall a little
  • Break-even ≈ stock cost basis − premium per share
  • If the stock finishes below the strike, you keep the shares and the premium

Green = profit · Red = loss · Flat right side = short call caps upside

Worked example: the P/L at different prices

You buy 100 shares at $50 and sell the $55 call for $1.00 → $100 premium. Per-share results (×100 for contract totals):

  • Stock at $40: shares −$10, premium +$1 → net −$9/share (−$900).
  • Stock at $49 (break-even): −$1 + $1 → ≈ $0.
  • Stock at $55: +$5 + $1 → +$6/share (+$600), the max-profit point.
  • Stock at $60: shares called away at $55 — same +$600. Everything above the strike is gone; that is the cap.

Formulas: break-even = $50 − $1 = $49; max profit = ($55 − $50) + $1 = $6/share ($600) if called away.

Choosing the strike and expiry

  • OTM vs ATM: an OTM call leaves room to rise but pays less; an ATM call pays more but caps you immediately.
  • Expiry: 30–45 days is a popular compromise — real theta decay, repeatable monthly. Weeklies pay less but let you adjust strikes often.
  • IV and dividends: higher IV means richer premiums. Near an ex-dividend date, a deep ITM call can be exercised early so the holder captures the dividend.

Managing the position: rolling up and out

  • Stock rallies past the strike: buy back the call and sell a higher-strike, later-expiry call — ideally for a net credit ("roll up and out").
  • Stock drops: the call decays and you keep the premium, but the shares are down. Sell another call next cycle — not below your cost basis.
  • Closing early & assignment: if the call has decayed to 10–20% of its sale price, buying it back banks most of the profit. If it finishes in the money, expect the shares to be called away — usually the weekend after expiry.

Common covered call mistakes

  • Selling calls on shares bought far higher, then accepting assignment below cost basis.
  • Chasing the richest premium on a volatile stock — the big premium prices the big move that makes the cap hurt most.
  • Forgetting dividends: selling an ITM call over an ex-date and losing the dividend to early exercise.
  • Thinking the premium “protects” the position — it offsets a few percent of a drop, nothing more.
Example: You own 100 shares at $50. You sell a $55 call for $1.00 ($100 credit).
If stock stays under $55: you keep shares + $100 premium.
If stock surges to $60: shares may be called at $55; you still keep the $100 premium, but you miss gains above $55.

When it fits / when it does not

  • Fits: you already like owning the shares; you want income; you accept capped upside
  • Does not fit: you expect a large rally soon; you cannot tolerate stock drawdowns; you do not own the shares (that would be a naked short call — different and much riskier)

Beginner checklist

  • Confirm you own 100 shares per call sold
  • Know the strike and expiration before you sell
  • Treat premium as a cushion, not a guarantee against large losses
  • Assignment can happen — especially near expiration if the call is in the money

Beginner FAQ

What is a covered call strategy?
A covered call means you own at least 100 shares and sell one call against those shares. You collect a premium and agree to sell the shares at the strike if assigned.

What is the max profit on a covered call?
Approximate max profit is (call strike minus stock cost) plus the premium received, if shares are called away or finish above the strike.

What is the covered call break-even?
Break-even is about stock cost basis minus premium per share, before commissions.

What if the stock drops far below my cost?
You still own the falling shares — the premium only offsets a few percent. Covered calls fit stocks you are comfortable holding through drawdowns.

When can I be assigned on a covered call?
Any time with American-style options, most often near expiry when the call is in the money — or early around an ex-dividend date on a deep in-the-money call.

What does "rolling up and out" mean?
Buying back your short call and selling a higher-strike, later-expiry call — ideally for a net credit — to stay in the stock and raise the cap.
