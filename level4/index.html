Level 4 · Longer horizons

Calendars, credits, covered calls & LEAPS

More structure, more time. Many discretionary traders prefer 45+ days to expiry so theta is slower at entry and there is room to manage.

Why target 45+ DTE?

  • Slower early decay — theta is often gentler far from expiry than in the final 2 weeks.
  • Room to adjust — roll, close, or hedge before gamma spikes near expiration.
  • LEAPS (long-dated options, often 1+ year) behave more like leveraged stock views with time still embedded.
This is a common teaching guideline, not a rule. Weekly options and 0-DTE products exist and behave very differently (high gamma, fast theta).

Theta decay — sample shape

Debit vs credit

Debit spreads vs credit spreads

Debit spread

You pay net premium (e.g. bull call, bear put). Max loss ≈ debit. Max gain ≈ width − debit.

Bull call (debit) → Bear put (debit) →

Credit spread

You receive net premium (e.g. bull put, bear call). Max gain ≈ credit. Max loss ≈ width − credit.

Example idea: sell $95 put / buy $90 put for a credit — defined risk if the stock stays above the short strike.

Calendar spread (horizontal)

Same strike, different expiries: typically sell a nearer-term option and buy a longer-dated one (net debit). You want the front month to decay faster while the back month keeps value — often best when price stays near the strike and IV doesn’t crush the long leg unfairly.

Stock + short call

Covered call (income overlay)

Own 100 shares, sell 1 call against them. You collect premium (“rent”) and cap upside above the short strike. Downside on the shares remains — the premium only cushions losses slightly.

  • Often used when neutral-to-mildly bullish on a stock you already hold
  • Prefer liquid strikes and enough DTE that you are paid for the risk (many use ~30–45 DTE as a teaching range)

Long-dated

LEAPS strategies

LEAPS are long-term equity anticipation securities — options with expirations often a year or more out. Traders use them to:

  • Express a multi-month/year directional view with less capital than 100 shares (still can lose 100% of premium)
  • Sell shorter calls against a LEAPS long call (a diagonal / poor-man’s covered call style idea)
Buy a 12–18 month call (LEAPS) as a surrogate for stock; optionally sell nearer calls against it to reduce cost basis — manage early assignment and dividend risk carefully in live markets.
