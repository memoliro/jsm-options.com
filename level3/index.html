Level 3 · Defined risk

Vertical spreads & the iron condor

Buy one option, sell another — cut cost or define risk. Then combine both sides into a market-neutral iron condor.

Why spreads?

A naked long call is simple but you pay full premium. A naked short call can have large risk. A vertical spread pairs a buy and a sell in the same expiry so max loss (and often max gain) are capped.

Builder premiums in templates are illustrative — edit them to match a live option chain before you treat numbers as realistic.
Bullish · defined risk

Bull call spread

Buy a lower-strike call, sell a higher-strike call (same expiry). Net debit. You want the stock up, but you cap upside at the short strike.

Example shape: Buy $100 call, sell $110 call. Net debit might be ~$4 ($400). Max gain roughly width − debit ≈ $6 ($600) if stock ≥ $110 at expiry. Max loss = debit paid.

Well below $100Both calls ~0 → lose net debit

Bearish · defined risk

Bear put spread

Buy a higher-strike put, sell a lower-strike put. Net debit. You profit if the stock falls, with max gain capped at the short put strike.

Buy $100 put, sell $90 put. Debit might be ~$4. Max value of spread = $10 width. Max loss = debit.

Neutral · time & range

Iron condor

Sell an OTM put spread and an OTM call spread (same expiry). Net credit. You want the stock to stay between the short strikes until expiry so both sides decay.

  • Max profit ≈ net credit if price stays in the “tent”
  • Max loss ≈ width of the tested wing − credit (still defined, not unlimited)
  • Risk rises if the stock trends hard or volatility explodes against you
Short put at $90 / long put at $80 + short call at $110 / long call at $120. Credit collected upfront; profit if price stays roughly $90–$110.
