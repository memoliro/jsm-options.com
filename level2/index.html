Long Call, Long Put, Covered Call — With Real Numbers.

Level 1 gave you the idea. Now let's trade: why $300 can control $10,000 of stock, and where the risk really hides.

Bullish • Unlimited upside

Long Call

You pay premium, you profit if stock goes up beyond Strike + Premium.

Ex: Stock current price is $100. Buy 1 Call option contract of Strike $105 for $3 (contract price) that expires in 30 days. Stock rises, closes at $110 at the end of Expiry day: Profit/ Loss = $110 (closing price)-$105 (strike price)-$3 (premium paid) = $2 × 100 = $200.

Risk: limited to $300 premium (if stock closing price is lower than $105(strike price) then contract expires worthless. Loss = $300). Reward: unlimited (if stock price go to the Moon).

Bearish • Profits when falls

Long Put

Right to SELL at strike. You profit when stock crashes.

Ex: Stock current price is $100. Buy 1 Put option contract of Strike $95 for $2.50 (contract price) that expires in 30 days. Stock falls, closes at $80 at the end of Expiry day: Profit/ Loss = $95 (strike price)-$80 (closing price)-$2.50 (premium paid) = $12.50 × 100 = $1250.

Used as insurance or directional bet.

Income • Own 100 shares

Covered Call — The Rent Strategy

You own 100 shares of AAPL at $100. You sell a Call at $110 for $2. You keep $200 rent. If AAPL stays below $110, you keep rent + shares. If it goes to $120, you must sell at $110 — you miss extra upside but you still profited.

  • Best when you think stock will stay flat or go up a little
  • Risk: if stock crashes, you still own it — you lose on shares

Quick Math: Why Options Are ×100

1 contract = 100 shares. Premium quoted per share, but you pay ×100. So $3 premium = $300 debit. P/L chart uses ×100 — Green = profit, Red = loss, just like in Builder.
