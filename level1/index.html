What is an option? Think of it as a reservation with a deadline.

You pay a fee today for a right that may be useful later. The fee is the premium, the agreed price is the strike, and the deadline is the expiration.

Call = right to buy

Imagine a $100 item. You pay $3 to reserve the right to buy it for $100 later. If the item is worth $130 at expiration, the right has $30 of intrinsic value. After the $3 premium, the expiration profit is $27 per share.

Call breakeven: strike + premium. With a $100 strike and $3 premium, breakeven is $103.

Put = right to sell

Imagine you own something worth $100 and pay $3 for protection that lets you sell it for $100 later. If its value falls to $70 at expiration, the put has $30 of intrinsic value. After the $3 premium, the expiration profit is $27 per share.

Put breakeven: strike − premium. With a $100 strike and $3 premium, breakeven is $97.

The five words to remember

  • Underlying: the stock or other asset the option refers to.
  • Strike: the contractual exercise price.
  • Premium: the option price, quoted per share in standard equity options.
  • Expiration: the date/time after which the contract no longer has time remaining.
  • Call vs Put: a call gives the holder the right to buy; a put gives the holder the right to sell.
